# Research brief

Collected 2026-11-05 by research agents. Vendor facts change quickly, and clinical wording needs a clinician. Confidence labels are the agents' own.

## Realtime multimodal AI options for a phone-camera emergency guidance app: Gemini Live, OpenAI Realtime, medical and open source vision models, model choice and session cost

Only Gemini Live takes a documented continuous camera feed, at a maximum of 1 FPS. OpenAI Realtime accepts still images only and has no video timeline. Independent tests put both near 1 second to first audio, audio only, and nobody has published numbers with video. Neither is fast enough to anchor AR or judge compressions, so those must run on the phone. Recommend gemini-3.8-live as primary, gpt-realtime-2.1 over WebRTC as fallback, and a scripted on-device protocol that works offline as the floor. A 10 minute session costs about $0.70 to $1.30 on Gemini and about $1.00 on gpt-realtime-2.1 if caching holds, so cost should not decide this. Skip fine-tuning for the MVP: neither vendor's realtime models can be tuned, and MedGemma has no published latency and does not target bystander scenes. Main blocker: Gemini API terms bar providing medical advice and under-18 audiences, and OpenAI restricts tailored licensed advice. Get written clarity before any public launch. No source shows how accurately any model reads CPR or AED scenes, so an eval set comes first.

### Recommendation

PRIMARY: gemini-3.8-live, not the extended-thinking variant. It is the only GA option with documented continuous camera frames, plus async tools, compression and resumption. Connect from the iPhone over WSS with a locked ephemeral token, media_resolution LOW. Send frames on demand rather than a constant 1 FPS: burst around scene checks and questions, otherwise one every 5 to 10 s. Keep compression and resumption on, handle GoAway, and add a watchdog for silence and runaway audio. Treat the Firebase Swift SDK as unproven for this model until a spike confirms it. Otherwise use raw URLSessionWebSocketTask.

FALLBACK: gpt-realtime-2.1 at minimal reasoning, over WebRTC, with input_image snapshots. It gives a separate failure domain, better expected behaviour on poor networks, a 60 minute session, a cacheable client secret, and a sideband for the Sentinel. Fail over on a 1011 or 1008 close, a failed resume, or first audio over about 3 s twice in a row (my suggested threshold, tune it). Compare gpt-realtime-2.1-mini in the bake-off, but only at minimal reasoning, since high measured 4.28 s.

FLOOR, no network: a scripted on-device protocol with pre-recorded voice, a local metronome and direct 911 dialing. This must work with no model at all.

ARCHITECTURE this implies: a fast loop on the phone (AR anchors from on-device body and object tracking, metronome, compression rate, PPG signal, step state machine) and a slow cloud loop (scene and resource recognition, choosing the step, answering questions, voice). Give the model tools such as advance_step, show_overlay(target), start_metronome and log_event, and have it speak approved protocol text. Overlays are drawn by on-device tracking, never from model coordinates. Send PPG as low-rate summary numbers through context updates, not raw samples. Build the EMS report from the app's own event log, not model transcripts. Run the Sentinel as a post-hoc checker that can trigger a client-side interruption and a spoken correction. A cheap model such as gemini-3.5-flash-lite adds about $0.03 per session. Do not describe it as preventing hallucinations, since it can only catch them after speech begins.

COST of a 10 minute session (my model, not vendor figures). Assumptions: 30 model turns, 3 min user speech, 4 min model speech, 3,000 token prompt and tools, 60 frames unless stated. Excludes reasoning tokens and Sentinel.
- gemini-3.8-live, headline per-minute prices with no context re-billing at 1 FPS: $0.14. This is a floor and unlikely.
- gemini-3.8-live, 60 frames, context re-billed each turn with no cache: $0.69 to $0.87 (70 versus 258 tokens per frame).
- gemini-3.8-live, 600 frames at 1 FPS, same basis: $1.30 to $2.58.
- gpt-realtime-2.1, 60 frames at an assumed 692 tokens: $0.97 if caching holds, $7.21 with no cache hits.
- gpt-realtime-2.1-mini, same: $0.23 if caching holds, $1.69 with no cache hits.
- gpt-live-1: $0.50 plus backend, and no native vision.
Budget about $1.50 per session. The ranking is uncertain: Artificial Analysis, using a fixed benchmark rather than this long-session model, shows the opposite order (3.8 Live $0.84 per hour of input audio, 2.1-mini $3.45 to $4.60, 2.1 $10.75 to $11.31). Log usage_metadata and response.done to settle it. At these levels cost should not drive the choice.

MEDICAL AND OPEN MODELS: do not use MedGemma or any fine-tuned LLM in the MVP. No vendor's realtime model can be tuned, MedGemma targets radiology, pathology, dermatology and fundus images with no latency data, and validation burden applies to any adaptation. Spend the effort instead on the system prompt, tool design, guideline-derived scripts and a labelled eval set. If training is needed later, train a small on-device detector for things like AED pads, not an LLM.

FIRST SPIKE, about 2 days: an iPhone harness that runs both providers over cellular. Measure token fetch to first audio, end of speech to first audio with frames flowing, reconnect during a live session, and cost per session. Score both on a small eval set of photos and video: AED recognition, pad placement, hand position on a manikin, and available-tool identification. Also test proactive audio and VAD with a speakerphone metronome and someone counting aloud. Let that result confirm or overturn the primary pick.

CHANGE OF PICK: if this becomes a public product and Google will not confirm in writing that first-aid guidance is permitted, promote OpenAI to primary and accept snapshot-only vision, subject to OpenAI's own licensed-professional condition and legal review.

### Blockers

- Terms: as written, Gemini API terms bar providing medical advice, and Google Cloud section 20(e) bars use as a substitute for professional medical advice, with no first-aid carve-out. A CPR coach is plausibly in scope, so a public launch on Gemini needs written confirmation or legal sign-off. OpenAI's policy conditions tailored licensed advice on appropriate licensed professional involvement, so it also needs review. This is not legal advice.
- Age: Gemini API terms bar services directed at or likely to be accessed by under-18s. A public emergency app could easily reach teenagers, so this needs resolving alongside the medical restriction.
- As stated, the VLM pointing at body parts will not work: frames arrive at no more than 1 FPS and replies are roughly 1 to 2 s old while the camera moves. AR placement must come from on-device tracking, with the model only choosing what to highlight.
- Cloud video cannot measure compression rate: 100 to 120 per minute needs more than 4 FPS and Live allows 1 FPS. Rate feedback and the metronome must be local.
- OpenAI has no native video input and no timeline, so a continuous camera feed is not available there. A 1 FPS image stream would exhaust the 128,000 token context in about 3 minutes at the assumed image size.
- A Sentinel cannot prevent a hallucination from being spoken with native speech-to-speech models, because audio already heard cannot be retracted and gemini-3.8-live outputs audio only. It can only correct afterwards unless the design moves to text plus separate TTS, at added latency.
- The Gemini free tier cannot be used with real people's video: content may be used to improve products and read by human reviewers, and Google says not to submit personal data.
- Gemini Live zero data retention requires no session resumption, while connections last about 10 minutes, so a retention-free session cannot simply resume past that limit.

### Open questions

- Is this a hackathon demo or a product, iOS only or cross-platform, and on what timeline? This decides how much the terms blockers matter now.
- How accurately do these models read bystander scenes? I found no published accuracy for AED recognition, pad placement or hand position from any of them, so this needs our own eval set.
- What is real end-of-speech to first-audio latency with frames flowing, on cellular? All independent figures found are audio only.
- Does the gemini-3.8-live model string work through Firebase AI Logic Swift 13.1.0? Its model list omits it, and the preview models it lists have an earliest shutdown of 2026-11-17.
- What does a Gemini 3.8 Live video frame actually cost: $0.002/min, 70 tokens per frame, or 258 tokens per frame?
- Does 3.8 Live re-bill or cache replayed context? Staff said no caching in July 2026, while newer docs report cached tokens in usage metadata.
- What image token count does gpt-realtime-2.1 charge per frame? OpenAI publishes no rule for realtime models.
- Will proactive audio ignore panicked or off-axis speech, and will a speakerphone metronome or someone counting aloud cause false barge-ins or block replies?
- How frequent are the 1011 and 1008 drops and failed resumes on 3.8 Live? The evidence is one developer's report, with no root cause posted.
- Can the Gemini token start window be extended enough to pre-mint before an emergency? The default is 1 minute and I did not find the maximum.
- Is Realtime covered under an OpenAI BAA, and is gemini-3.8-live on Agent Platform covered by Google's BAA, if PHI handling turns out to apply?

### Findings

- **high:** Gemini Live model IDs: gemini-3.8-live and gemini-3.8-live-extended-thinking are GA (released 2026-09-15) with no shutdown announced. gemini-3.1-flash-live-preview and gemini-2.5-flash-native-audio-preview-12-2025 are preview with an earliest shutdown of 2026-11-17, 12 days from today, so do not build on them. ([source](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/deprecations)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** OpenAI realtime model IDs currently listed: gpt-live-1, gpt-realtime-2.1, gpt-realtime-2.1-mini, gpt-realtime-2, gpt-realtime-1.5. gpt-realtime-2.1 and its mini have a 128,000 token context and 32,000 max output. ([source](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** Gemini Live video is a stream of individual JPEG or PNG frames, 1 FPS maximum, 768x768 recommended. On 3.8 the default turn coverage includes every frame sent, so Google advises sending frames only when needed. Firebase docs say fast-moving video is a poor fit. Live has no separate video format. ([source](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/live-api/capabilities)) (model page updated Sept 2026; accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** OpenAI Realtime has no native video input. gpt-realtime-2.1 takes text, audio and image, sent as input_image items through conversation.item.create. OpenAI Support said on 2026-09-09 that native video streaming has no release timeline. gpt-live-1 takes no image input at all (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-live-1). ([source](https://community.openai.com/t/when-will-gpt-realtime-2-1-with-live-video-input-be-released-in-the-api/1392430)) (2026-09-09)
- **low:** A 2025 test found that attaching a WebRTC video track to gpt-realtime produced snapshots rather than a stream, and one 640x360 image cost about $0.0675, which the author could not explain. This path is undocumented and old. Use input_image, not a video track. ([source](https://webrtchacks.com/how-openai-does-webrtc-in-the-new-gpt-realtime/)) (2025-09-23)
- **high:** Realtime audio: Gemini takes raw 16-bit little-endian PCM at 16 kHz and returns 24 kHz, streamed in 20 to 100 ms chunks. OpenAI over WebRTC carries audio on media tracks and JSON events on a data channel. ([source](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/guides/gemini-3-8-live)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** Barge-in: on Gemini, server VAD is on by default, an interruption cancels and discards generation, and pending function calls are discarded with their IDs reported. Google recommends 500 to 800 ms silence_duration (default about 800 ms). On OpenAI, VAD cancels the response, and WebRTC and SIP truncate unplayed audio automatically, while a WebSocket client must do this itself (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime-conversations). ([source](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/live-api/capabilities)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** Safety-relevant behaviour: proactive audio is always on for gemini-3.8-live and cannot be disabled. The model filters ambient and background speech and answers only when addressed, so it may stay silent in a chaotic scene. The app can force a reply with send_client_content and turn_complete=true, which unconditionally interrupts generation. Whether this is safe with panicked speech is untested. ([source](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/guides/gemini-3-8-live)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** Gemini tool calling during a live session: 3.8 Live defaults to NON_BLOCKING, with scheduling of INTERRUPT, WHEN_IDLE or SILENT. An INTERRUPT response sent while the user speaks is downgraded to WHEN_IDLE, so a correction cannot cut in while a bystander counts aloud. BLOCKING calls are cancelled by new input. Tool responses need status and retry fields or the model may repeat calls. Extended-thinking is async only, and a forum report shows it closing with 1007 when given scheduling. ([source](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/live-api/migrate-from-gemini-2-5-to-gemini-3-8-live)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **medium:** OpenAI tool calling: function calls arrive in response.done and results return via function_call_output plus response.create. A server-side sideband WebSocket can attach to a running WebRTC session to read transcripts, run tools and inject instructions, which suits a Sentinel agent. I found no documented sideband for a direct phone-to-Gemini WebSocket, so a Gemini Sentinel needs the phone to relay transcripts or a server media pipeline. ([source](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-server-controls)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** A Sentinel cannot un-say audio. OpenAI states corrective instructions cannot retract what the caller already heard. gemini-3.8-live supports audio as its only response modality, so pre-speech screening is not possible there. gpt-realtime can output text only (output_modalities) for screening before separate TTS, at added latency. LiveKit notes the text-plus-TTS hybrid does not work with native-audio Gemini models. ([source](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-server-controls)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **medium:** Independent time-to-first-audio from Artificial Analysis, in seconds, audio only: gemini-3.8-live 1.18; extended thinking high 1.35; gpt-realtime-2.1 minimal 0.97, high 1.21; gpt-realtime-2.1-mini minimal 0.85, high 4.28; gpt-live-1 1.24 to 1.34; gpt-realtime-1.5 0.81. So keep reasoning effort low on OpenAI. The page gives no measurement date and I could not retrieve its methodology. No source measures latency with video frames in the session. ([source](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-speech)) (undated, copyright 2026; accessed 2026-11-05)
- **medium:** Google describes 3.8 Live as designed for sub-second latency, but that is a vendor claim and the independent figure is 1.18 s. If that figure excludes VAD, adding the 500 to 800 ms end-of-speech silence gives my estimate of 1.5 to 2 s from end of speech to first audible word. Latency can also regress: a forum report describes 9 to 15 s first-audio delay and unanswered turns on 3.1 Flash Live from 2026-09-05 (https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-3-1-flash-live-preview-9-15-s-time-to-first-audio-since-2026-09-05-09-00-utc-was-1-s-some-turns-never-answer-reproduced-from-two-projects-two-networks/180918). ([source](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/guides/gemini-3-8-live)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** Gemini session limits: without compression, 15 minutes audio-only and only 2 minutes audio plus video, and exceeding them ends the session. Connections last about 10 minutes, with a GoAway about 60 s before (https://firebase.google.com/docs/ai-logic/live-api/limits-and-specs). Context window compression allows unlimited duration, and resumption handles stay valid 2 hours after termination. A 10 minute session will likely cross a reconnect, so both features must be on from day one. ([source](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/live-api/session-management)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **medium:** OpenAI Realtime sessions last at most 60 minutes, so a 10 minute emergency needs no planned reconnect. There is no server-side resume for a dropped session: SDK docs say each reconnect starts a new server session that the app must restore. GPT-Live can only fork a stored session or restart from saved text (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live-conversations). ([source](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime-conversations)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **medium:** Reliability evidence for 3.8 Live is thin but concerning. One developer reported on 2026-10-20 mid-conversation 1011 closes where resume then failed with 1008, reply audio running up to about 86 s after a 10 s sentence, and output transcripts containing words never spoken. Google asked for logs and no root cause is posted. The system was voice only. Implication: add a watchdog and do not build the EMS report from model transcripts. ([source](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-3-8-live-1011-1008-session-drops-runaway-audio-and-output-transcription-with-unspoken-text/190881)) (2026-10-20, Google reply 2026-10-30)
- **high:** Transports: Gemini Live directly is a stateful WebSocket only, and WebRTC comes through partners (LiveKit, Pipecat, Fishjam, Agora and others). OpenAI recommends WebRTC for mobile and also offers WebSocket and SIP, with WARP field trials in libwebrtc to shorten session start, though it publishes no savings figures (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime-webrtc-warp). WebRTC should handle poor cellular better than TCP WebSocket, but I found no measured comparison. ([source](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/live-api)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** Gemini ephemeral tokens: minted by your backend at POST v1beta/auth_tokens. expireTime defaults to 30 minutes and newSessionExpireTime to 1 minute. uses:1 still permits resumption reconnects, and liveConnectConstraints can lock the model and config. WebSocket and v1beta only. The short start window limits pre-minting, so a backend round trip may sit in the emergency start path unless a longer window is allowed. ([source](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/live-api/ephemeral-tokens)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** OpenAI ephemeral tokens: POST /v1/realtime/client_secrets, TTL 10 to 7200 s, default 600. A running session can outlive the secret and one secret can start multiple sessions, so it can be cached ahead of an emergency. Session options can be overridden by the client, so they are not locked as on Gemini. GPT-Live uses a different flow where your server creates the session and relays SDP (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-webrtc). ([source](https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/resources/realtime/subresources/client_secrets/methods/create)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **medium:** iOS for Gemini: Firebase AI Logic for Swift has Live support, including video send, resumption, compression and Gemini 3.x voice activity (SDK 13.1.0, 2026-10-21). Qwen3 Omni Realtime is hosted by Alibaba, with 0.88 s TTFA on Artificial Analysis and a third-party plugin defaulting to 1 FPS. I could not verify its video limits or license, and would not send patient video there without review. ([source](https://pypi.org/project/vision-agents-plugins-qwen/0.6.2/)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **medium:** Specialized EMS research does not close the gap. CognitiveEMS (AR glasses plus edge compute, with fine-tuned speech and small language models) reports 3.78 s end-to-end protocol prediction on the edge versus 0.31 s on a server, and 0.727 action recognition accuracy. CPR-Coach (CVPR 2024) is a public dataset of 13 single and 74 composite CPR error actions, a possible later source for an on-device classifier. I did not verify that it fits phone-angle video. ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06734)) (2024)
- **high:** Fine-tuning cannot enter the realtime loop on either vendor. The Gemini API has no tunable models and no immediate plans (https://aistudio.google.com/docs/model-tuning). Tuning remains on Agent Platform, with no statement about Live. OpenAI lists fine-tuning as not supported for gpt-realtime-2 and gpt-live-1. A tuned model could only be an out-of-band tool call, adding latency. ([source](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-realtime-2)) (accessed 2026-11-05)

## Anchoring AR overlays on a human body and physical objects from a phone camera with low latency (CPR hand position, AED, on-device vs cloud, stack, hands-free)

Build the overlay on-device; the cloud cannot drive it. Gemini Live takes at most 1 frame per second, and general vision models take seconds per image. OpenAI's own docs say its models struggle with precise spatial localization. Use native Swift with ARKit world tracking and a 2D pose model (Apple Vision or a YOLO pose model via Core ML) running on rotated crops. Compute the hand target from the four torso keypoints. I found no evidence that ARKit body tracking works on a supine person. MediaPipe BlazePose is a poor fit: its model card excludes head-not-visible cases and life-critical use, and it scored OKS 0.39 on supine infants versus about 0.87-0.92 for other methods. Nobody has published validated sternum localization from a phone, so treat the overlay as a coarse aid that never delays compressions. WebXR is not supported on iOS. Hands-free is possible: lone lay responders propped phones on the ground about 1 m away, at a cost of roughly 30 s. That low side view is poor for pose estimation, and the rescuer cannot watch a screen while compressing. So AR is for the moment before compressions, and audio carries the compressions themselves.

### Recommendation

STACK: iOS-first native Swift (SwiftUI shell, RealityKit ARView, ARSession with ARWorldTrackingConfiguration, horizontal plane detection, sceneDepth when supported, gravity alignment). The machine already has Xcode. Do not use ARBodyTrackingConfiguration as the anchor source, WebXR, or RN for the AR loop. If Android is needed later, use ARCore plus LiteRT or MediaPipe behind the same logic.

POSE: run a one-day device bake-off before committing. Compare Apple Vision 2D (no dependency, multiple poses), yolo26n-pose via Core ML, ARBodyTrackingConfiguration, and BlazePose as controls, on real lying adults. Views: high above chest, kneeling oblique, low side at 1 m, head out of frame, clothed. Suggested pass bar (my proposal): acquire within 2 s, target error within about 4.5 cm of a human-labeled point in 90% of frames. Expect to fine-tune on lying-person data, with custom keypoints if useful.

RUNS ON-DEVICE PER FRAME, no network: ARKit camera pose (every frame); latest-frame-wins pose inference at about 15-30 FPS; crop rotated by the previous torso axis, trying 0/90/180/270 on cold start; patient selection by horizontal torso using gravity; homography target; 1 Euro smoothing; overlay rendering; metronome audio. AED: custom YOLO Core ML detector at a low rate plus VNTrackObjectRequest between detections.

RUNS IN THE CLOUD, at most 1 FPS or on demand, never gating the overlay: Gemini Live voice and scene understanding; one-shot resource inventory ('what tools are here'); zero-shot points for unknown equipment, repeated and averaged; Sentinel cross-check that compares the cloud's point to the on-device ring and hides the ring on disagreement. Scripted on-device voice protocol if offline.

HOW THE OVERLAY STAYS ATTACHED, as a state machine. TRACKING: draw the ring from live keypoints each frame, which follows a moving body and needs no depth. LOCKED: when confidence is high, write a world anchor using LiDAR depth, or floor plane plus body-height offset, or two-view triangulation; when hands occlude the torso, hold this anchor, which ARKit keeps stable at frame rate as the phone moves. STALE: if tracking is limited, or the anchor is old or disagrees with fresh keypoints, hide the ring and switch to voice ('centre of the chest'). Show a coarse palm-sized region, never a precise dot. For delayed cloud answers: keep a ledger of frame id to camera transform and intrinsics, tag each uploaded JPEG, compute the ray from the stored pose, intersect with depth or plane, then project with the current camera. Copy those values out rather than retaining ARFrames (from my experience, not verified in docs). Latency target to measure, my estimate: about 60-80 ms for keypoint-driven overlays, with world-locked content at display rate.

HANDS-FREE FLOW: speaker on immediately. Give at most a few seconds of AR hand placement if the phone is already pointed at the patient; if pose is not acquired fast, go straight to voice. Never ask a lone rescuer to prop the phone before starting compressions, given the roughly 30 s cost. During compressions use audio metronome and voice. Propped phone at about 1 m can estimate rate from arm motion; do not promise depth feedback, since humans judged depth correctly only 32% of the time from such video. If a second person exists, have them hold the phone high above the patient's legs, and use them for pads while CPR continues.

### Blockers

- Driving per-frame AR anchors from a cloud VLM will not work: Gemini Live input is at most 1 FPS and general vision models take seconds per image.
- A chest overlay as live guidance during compressions for a lone rescuer will not work as imagined: both hands are busy, arms occlude the torso, shoulders must be over the chest, and a phone on the ground 1 m away is not a usable display.
- Unsafe if the overlay or phone setup delays compressions: propping took about 23-34 s in the only lone-responder study, and guidelines say start as soon as possible. AR must never gate CPR.
- Precise sternum localization is unproven: I found no published validation from a phone on real people, and the sternum fraction here is my own derivation. Do not present it as accurate or as clinically validated.
- MediaPipe BlazePose as a foundation is a poor fit: its model card puts head-not-visible out of scope and says it is not intended for life-critical decisions, and it scored far below others on supine infants.
- ARKit body tracking as the anchor is unsupported by evidence for supine people, tracks one person, and has iOS 26 regression reports with no confirmed status on iOS 27.
- WebXR is not supported on iPhone Safari, so a web-only AR approach cannot serve iOS.
- Single-person models may lock on to the upright rescuer with a visible face rather than the patient, so patient selection logic is required (my inference from Vision 3D and BlazePose docs).
- Camera conflict across features: PPG with a finger on the lens blocks the rear camera, so it cannot run at the same moment as AR on one phone (my inference; coordinate with the PPG plan).

- **low:** ARKit body tracking: Apple's docs state no limit on the tracked person's pose or orientation, and my searches found no report, positive or negative, of it working on a supine person. Behaviour on a person lying flat, partly occluded, or seen from above is therefore UNVERIFIED and needs a device test. Known facts: rear camera only, tracks a single person, root is the hip joint. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arbodyanchor)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** ARKit motion capture requires A12 or later. The 3D skeleton has 91 joints, of which only some are estimated and the rest follow the nearest estimated parent. The 2D body has 16 landmarks. The session gives no guidance on orientation, occlusion, or distance. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/607/)) (2019-06)
- **low:** Apple's own sample code for placing a body anchor on a surface computes a hip-to-foot offset along Y, which assumes an upright body. This is a weak signal that the design did not target people lying down (my inference, not an Apple statement). ([source](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arbodyanchor)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **medium:** ARKit body tracking has regression history. Developers reported nil joint transforms after updating to iOS 26. One thread's poster says it was fixed in iOS 26.3 (Feb 2026, not confirmed by Apple). A second thread on iPhone 17 Pro Max and 15 Pro has no Apple reply and no reported fix. iOS 27 status not found. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/803714)) (2026-02)
- **medium:** Second iOS 26 body tracking thread (FB15128723): 0 valid joints per frame, reproduces in Apple's 'Capturing Body Motion in 3D' sample, no Apple response as of Dec 2025. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/804899)) (2025-12)
- **high:** Apple Vision 2D body pose (VNDetectHumanBodyPoseRequest, iOS 14+) returns an array of observed poses. Apple warns that accuracy drops when people are 'bent over or upside down', with flowing clothing, when one person partly occludes another, and near the frame edges. A supine person at arbitrary in-plane rotation falls close to the upside-down warning (my inference). ([source](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10653/)) (2020-06)
- **high:** Apple Vision 3D body pose (iOS 17+): 17 joints, works without ARKit, returns ONE skeleton for the most prominent person (the closest to the camera in Apple's example). Height falls back to a 1.8 m reference without depth metadata. No real-time or partial-body guidance is given. In a rescue the most prominent person may be the rescuer, not the patient (my inference). ([source](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/111241/)) (2023-06)
- **high:** MediaPipe BlazePose model card lists as out of scope: head not visible, multiple people in the image, people further than about 4 m, and applications needing metric depth. It says the model is 'sensitive to face position, scale and orientation', 'not intended for human life-critical decisions', and that its primary application is entertainment. Training attributes assume a full body in the crop and tolerate 8 degrees of roll. Pixel 3 speeds: lite ~44 FPS CPU / ~49 GPU, full ~18 / ~40, heavy ~4 / ~19. ([source](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20BlazePose%20GHUM%203D.pdf)) (2021-04-16)
- **medium:** BlazePose uses a face detector as a proxy for finding the person, assuming the head is visible. The detector runs on the first frame, then later frames track from previous keypoints. A covered face, or a head out of frame at close range, is therefore likely to fail cold start (the failure is my inference). ([source](https://research.google/blog/on-device-real-time-body-pose-tracking-with-mediapipe-blazepose/)) (2020)
- **high:** MediaPipe iOS: available in Swift, 33 landmarks, numPoses option (default 1), liveStream mode that drops frames when busy to keep results current. The docs do not state latency or orientation limits for iOS. ([source](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker/ios)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** Best direct evidence on supine pose, but in infants, so it transfers only partly to adults: on real supine infant images MediaPipe scored OKS 0.39 versus AlphaPose 0.87, Detectron2 0.87, OpenPose 0.87, HRNet 0.90-0.92, and ViTPose 0.92 (on video). Neck-MidHip error was 38% for MediaPipe versus 6-8% for the best others. Authors suspect its face-based detector. Errors rose when the camera was 'too low or too much on the side' compared with a view from above. ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17382v4)) (2025-09-11)
- **medium:** I found no peer-reviewed study validating MediaPipe, Vision, ARKit, or YOLO pose on supine adults from a phone against ground truth. In-bed literature says general-purpose models lose effectiveness on overhead lying views. Orientation rectification improved PCK0.1 by 26.4%, and fine-tuning added 16.6%. This supports rotating the crop and fine-tuning. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.01005v3)) (2019)
- **medium:** SLP lying-pose dataset: models trained on lying data reach about 94-97% PCKh@0.5 on LWIR and depth. RGB was excluded from the 2D benchmark, so this does not prove RGB performance. In 3D tests some RGB-based models 'completely fail the detection'. Fine-tuning on lying data looks like the most evidence-backed route to robustness. ([source](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2008.08735)) (2020)
- **medium:** Steep overhead views challenge real-time pose models with low resolution, steep angles, and self-occlusion. Fine-tuning for aerial views gave +16.3 mAP on UAV-Human at about 20 ms on a Jetson Orin AGX. This is an analogue for an above view, not a study of lying people. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05747v1)) (2026 (WACV))
- **low:** Geometry insight (my inference, untested): looking straight down at a supine person and rotating the crop so the head is at the top resembles a frontal standing person, which is what models train on. The low side view is the hard case. So instruct the user to hold the phone high over the chest rather than low from the side. A kneeling rescuer at about 70 cm covers roughly 1 m along the long edge (assuming about 70 degree FOV, not verified), so expect torso only with the head and legs often out of frame.
- **high:** Target definition: RCUK 2025 says heel of one hand on the lower half of the sternum ('in the centre of the chest'), that garments may be displaced or removed to identify the landmark, and that it matters more that people act than focus on small details. ([source](https://www.resus.org.uk/print/pdf/node/36437)) (2025-10-27)
- **medium:** AHA 2025 Part 7 says to place the heel over the center of the chest, roughly lower half to lower third of the sternum, and may consider the dominant hand on the sternum (limited evidence). ILCOR 2025 keeps lower half of sternum as a weak recommendation on very low certainty evidence. I read these only via search excerpts because the full pages returned 403. Verify exact wording. ([source](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001369)) (2025)
- **medium:** CT anatomy for mapping keypoints to the sternum (204 Thai adults): sternal length 186.7 mm (F 174.2, M 200.1). Inter-nipple line is 84.5 mm above the xiphoid tip, 45.1% of length (arms raised). Adjusted maximal LV level is 89.7 mm, 48.1% of length. Authors recommend the upper part of the lower half, the second quarter from the xiphoid. Limits: Thai population, static CT, not in arrest. Half of the sternum is about 9 cm, so along-axis tolerance is about +/-4.5 cm (my derivation). ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10733391/)) (2023)
- **low:** Proposed CPR target algorithm (my derivation, NOT validated anywhere): use the left and right shoulder and hip keypoints as a planar quad, fit a homography to a unit torso square, and place the target at u=0.5 and v of about 0.2-0.25 from the shoulder line. Rationale: if the notch is near the shoulder line, the target is about 52% of an 18.7 cm sternum (about 10 cm) below it, with shoulder-to-hip assumed at 45-55 cm (assumption; I could not verify anthropometric coefficients). The homography handles oblique views better than a midpoint. Close-up fallback when hips are out of frame: offset of about 0.25-0.3 of shoulder width perpendicular to the shoulder line, at lower confidence. Calibrate v on labeled images.
- **medium:** I found no peer-reviewed validation of markerless smartphone hand-position detection against a clinical reference. Closest is VISIOCPR (Sensors, May 2026), a webcam-only training system with a 40-participant RCT, read only via search snippets because the page returned 403, so its landmark method is unconfirmed. The AHA/ARCore Hands-Only CPR app overlays a VIRTUAL patient and does not detect a real person, so it does not prove the concept. ([source](https://ahahealthtech.org/new-augmented-reality-hands-only-cpr-makes-training-life-like-and-mobile)) (2018)
- **medium:** Hand placement is a real problem: a live-video dispatch study reported 42.2% (38/90) of bystanders had incorrect hand position, and 60.5% of those improved after video guidance (Resuscitation 2021 abstract, read via search snippet only). However a meta-analysis of mostly simulation trials found 78.3% vs 67.1% adequate hand position with video vs audio, pooled difference not significant. So voice instruction already gets most people close. ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8812740)) (2022)

The Robotics-ER page warns the models can hallucinate, especially with ambiguous prompts, and recommends querying multiple times and averaging for high-precision work. It gives no numeric latency or accuracy. I found no published accuracy for AED or body landmarks, so you must run your own evaluation. ([source](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/robotics-overview)) (accessed 2026-11-05)

This rules out per-frame anchoring from Live. ([source](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/live-api)) (accessed 2026-11-05)

The docs do not confirm spatial outputs in streaming mode. ER 1.6 is deprecated. ([source](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/models/gemini-robotics-er-2-streaming-preview)) (2026-07)

This is pooled across five tasks and is not a latency-tuned setup, so read it as 'seconds, not milliseconds' rather than a precise latency. Measure real round trips on your own network. ([source](https://playground.roboflow.com/models/compare/gemini-3-5-flash-vs-yolo-world)) (2026-10-26)

It does not name specific models for the limitation. A vendor benchmark reported GPT-4o mAP@0.5 of 0.02 at 5150 ms vs YOLOv8n 0.20 at 365 ms, but that is one setup, a weak source, and an old model. ([source](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/images-vision)) (accessed 2026-11-05)

These show the scale, not a YOLO figure. I found no official iPhone number for YOLO26. Community data: YOLOv8n at 24-25 ms on iPhone SE 3 via TFLite Core ML delegate, and a '30+ FPS' target for YOLO26 with no device named. Measure with Xcode's Core ML performance report. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/machine-learning/models/)) (accessed 2026-11-05)

Custom keypoint layouts can be trained, and Core ML export produces an .mlpackage. CPU ONNX 40.3 ms is not a phone figure. It is a multi-person model and has no stated face dependency, so it is a sensible challenger, and custom keypoints could directly label sternum and pad targets. ([source](https://docs.ultralytics.com/tasks/pose)) (accessed 2026-11-05)

SpatialTrackingSession (iOS 18+) can also run on an ARSession you supply. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/arview)) (accessed 2026-11-05)

Docs do not explicitly list which configurations support it, so check with supportsFrameSemantics on ARWorldTrackingConfiguration at runtime. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arframe/detectedbody)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **medium:** A floor-plane raycast misplaces a chest anchor. Assuming the chest is 25 cm above the floor and the camera 1 m up, the anchor lands 25 cm too low, and a 20 cm sideways phone move creates about 5 cm of overlay error at the chest (my calculation), which is about the whole along-axis tolerance. So use LiDAR sceneDepth where available, otherwise a floor plane plus a body-height offset, or triangulation across two camera poses.

([source](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcamera/trackingstate-swift.enum/reason)) (accessed 2026-11-05)

([source](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfiguration/worldalignment-swift.enum/gravity)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **medium:** Hands-free, direct evidence: lone lay responders (n=10, simulation pilot) all got a usable view (0/10 failures). Most phones sat on the ground about 1 m away, vertical, propped on a table leg, mug, shoe, stone, or brick, showing the patient from the side with arms in frame. Instruction to first compression was mean 23.4 s indoors and 34.0 s outdoors (the paper's table implies slightly higher). Instructors assessed hand position correctly 86%, rate 84%, hand placement 78%, depth only 32%. Very small study. ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12276233/)) (2025-07-19)
- **high:** A 2026 randomized simulation mounted the phone on a small inclined wooden stand beside a kneeling participant, with handheld use not permitted, angled to see the compression area. Authors flag that real handheld use, camera angle, and connection could limit this outside the lab. No significant difference in hand position or time to CPR between video and voice. ([source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/8C9A502C43B657D0543142589B5B308B/core-reader)) (2026-04-06)
- **high:** With a SECOND bystander filming, a protocol used three camera positions: in front of the rescuer, above the patient's legs at a high angle (shows hands and rhythm), and in front of the legs at a low angle (shows depth and recoil). Hands correct: 62.94% phone only, 71.36% video, 73.17% video plus protocol. Small simulation. Note the video-only group had worse rhythm and recoil than phone only. ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7175934)) (2020)
- **medium:** A rescuer cannot use a screen during compressions: guidance says arms straight with shoulders vertically above the chest, and RCUK says to use the phone's speaker function and start compressions as soon as possible. The torso is also occluded by the rescuer's hands and arms, and a low side view is the worst pose case. Therefore the overlay is useful before compressions or for a helper, and audio carries the compression phase (my synthesis). ([source](https://www.resus.org.uk/print/pdf/node/36437)) (2025-10-27)

## Smartphone fingertip camera PPG: accuracy, emergency safety, AR conflict on iPhone, and where it belongs in the app

Cut PPG from the emergency path. Fingertip camera PPG is validated only for still, cooperative adults: mean heart rate within a few bpm in resting sinus rhythm, and AF detection with good pooled numbers from weak studies. I found no study of phone-camera PPG in unconscious, arrested, shocked or cold patients. AHA 2025 tells lay rescuers not to rely on a pulse check: unresponsive plus abnormal breathing means start CPR, and ILCOR finds injury from CPR on non-arrest patients is rare. So a reading cannot improve that decision and can only harm it, through a false pulse (the rescuer's own finger, motion in the compression frequency band) or a 60-second recording. Hardware also blocks it: a finger over the lens removes the AR feed, and Apple says a second capture session interrupts ARKit, so the two can only run in sequence. The one defensible role is an optional, post-MVP heart-rate estimate for an awake patient, logged to the EMS report, never feeding the CPR decision and never lowering urgency. Cut post-ROSC and rescuer-stress uses. AF or rhythm claims need regulatory clearance.

### Recommendation

Cut PPG from the MVP and from every path where the patient is unresponsive or CPR is in progress. Spend the time saved on triage, the metronome and AR guidance. Do not stream PPG to the VLM to decide what to do. Treat the absence of a pulse check as a deliberate safety feature and say so in any pitch.

If PPG is kept at all, build it as an optional post-MVP module called something like "awake patient heart-rate estimate", with these hard rules:
1. Triage comes first and is deterministic: unresponsive plus not breathing normally goes to 911 and CPR. No PPG UI or code path is reachable from there, and no PPG value or model output can move a patient off that path (a Sentinel invariant).
2. The module opens only after the app has confirmed the patient is awake and talking and 911 has been offered.
3. Data flows one way, into the EMS report and dispatcher script, labeled as an unverified app estimate. It may raise concern but never lowers urgency, and it never produces a reassuring message.
4. Voice-confirm that it is the patient's finger, require 30-60 s of stillness, and on a failed quality check show no number at all, never zero or "no pulse". The 30-60 s window is my design choice, based on 60 s validated recordings; test it.
5. Show heart rate only. No AF or rhythm classification without regulatory clearance, and no SpO2 or blood pressure.
6. Run it in sequence: pause ARSession, run a plain AVCaptureSession with torch and a per-model finger diagram, then resume AR. Observe isTorchAvailable and system pressure.

For a hackathon demo, a 60-second reading on an awake volunteer is fine as a bonus, provided the CPR flow visibly excludes it. Have a clinician sign off on these rules before any real-world use.

### Blockers

- Using a PPG reading to decide whether to start or continue CPR will not work and is unsafe. It conflicts with AHA 2025 lay rescuer recognition, which does not rely on a pulse check, and a false pulse could withhold CPR.
- Sending PPG in real time to the general VLM to decide what to do, as stated in the idea, is unsafe in any arrest-capable flow. An LLM would be weighing an unvalidated signal against a guideline rule that needs no pulse input.
- Running PPG and rear-camera AR at the same moment will not work. A covered lens gives AR no view, the phone cannot be aimed at the chest while pressed to a finger, and Apple says a second AVCaptureSession interrupts ARKit. AVCaptureMultiCamSession would mean giving up ARKit and writing your own AR, with torch behavior unverified.
- PPG cannot be done by a lone rescuer during CPR: it needs hands, stillness and up to 60 s per reading.
- AF or rhythm claims are regulated. The leading cleared product, FibriCheck, is Class II, prescription only, and limited to self-testing by AF-prone patients. App Store 1.4.1 rejects accuracy claims that cannot be validated.
- Camera-only SpO2 or blood pressure would be rejected under App Store 1.4.1, so do not let them creep into this feature.

### Open questions

- Is this a hackathon demo or a product? A demo can include an awake-patient bonus; a product should defer PPG until a clinician and regulatory review have looked at it.
- Will any users be health care professionals? Their algorithm includes a pulse check of 10 seconds or less, but camera PPG is unvalidated for that role and the recording time alone exceeds it, so my recommendation does not change.
- Does a heart-rate estimate in an emergency app make it a regulated device even without rhythm claims? I could not verify 21 CFR 870.2790 or current FDA general wellness guidance (ecfr.gov blocked the fetch). This needs regulatory review.
- AHA full text could not be opened directly (403), so pulse-check findings rely on search excerpts of AHA pages. A clinician should confirm wording, COR/LOE and the lay post-ROSC guidance, which I could not retrieve.
- No phone-camera data exists that I could find for shock, cold hands or unconscious patients. If PPG stays, is a bench test (cold-water finger, simulated compression motion, rescuer's finger) worth running to quantify false-pulse rates?
- What frame rate holds on target iPhones with the torch on, and does the torch work inside a multi-cam session? Both need on-device testing.
- How much time does ARKit need to relocalize after a pause for PPG, and are placed anchors preserved? I did not test this.
- Android was out of scope here. If the app goes cross-platform, camera and torch concurrency need separate research.

### Findings

- **high:** Standard pipeline: fingertip over the rear lens with the LED torch on, about 30 fps video, per-frame channel intensity (red is the usual choice, but some methods weight and combine channels and drop saturated ones), baseline normalization or bandpass filtering, then peak, derivative or zero-crossing beat detection, plus a signal quality index. Quality gating is costly: at a 0.95 threshold about 56% of samples were discarded. A second source (Darmstadt 2017, 30 Hz, n=31) used bandpass plus zero-crossing: https://www.kom.tu-darmstadt.de/publications/GDG17-1 ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7181214)) (2020-03-30)
- **medium:** About 30 Hz is adequate for mean heart rate but contested for beat-to-beat analysis. Even with strict quality filtering, rMSSD and pNN50 stayed below acceptable agreement with ECG at about 30 Hz, and actual frame rate depended on the operating system. Camera PPG should not be sold as HRV or fine rhythm timing. ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7181214)) (2020-03-30)
- **medium:** Current iPhone hardware far exceeds the sampling PPG needs. iPhone 18 Pro lists 4K up to 120 fps and 1080p at 120 fps on the Fusion Main camera only, and slo-mo up to 240 fps at 1080p. These are marketing specs. I did not verify which AVCaptureDevice formats hold these rates with the torch on, so query videoSupportedFrameRateRanges on device and use real frame timestamps. Published phone PPG work mostly uses about 30 fps, so 60 fps is a sensible unvalidated target. ([source](https://www.apple.com/iphone-18-pro/specs/)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** Heart rate accuracy is good only in resting adults in sinus rhythm. A meta-analysis of 14 studies found a mean difference of -0.32 bpm (99% CI -1.24 to 0.60) and concluded agreement in that population, with pediatric use not validated (https://cardio.jmir.org/2018/1/e4, read via search excerpt; full text did not load). Coppetti, 108 patients on iPhone 4 and 5: contact apps had mean absolute error of 2 and 4.5 bpm against ECG, while pulse oximetry was 2 bpm. ([source](https://esc365.escardio.org/journal/29450)) (2017)
- **high:** Accuracy degrades in the rhythms that matter most. RELATION PPG (FibriCheck, 50 pre-ablation patients, unsupervised, own phones): error in sinus rhythm was -0.4 bpm with RMSE 2.2, but in AF it was -6.6 bpm with RMSE 11.8, limits about -25.8 to +12 bpm, and worse underestimation at high heart rates. AF sensitivity fell to 85.2% in bradycardia. 10.5% of first-attempt recordings were unusable even in these motivated patients. ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11023210/)) (2024-04)
- **high:** AF detection looks strong on paper but rests on weak evidence. O'Sullivan 2020 (10 studies, 3,852 participants): sensitivity 94.2%, specificity 95.8%, but modeled PPV was only 19.3% at 1.3% prevalence, and most studies were manufacturer-linked. Gill 2022 (28 studies) pooled about 94%/97% and judged studies small and biased, with none meeting all QUADAS-2 criteria: https://heart.bmj.com/content/108/20/1600 ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7125433/)) (2020-04-03)
- **medium:** Best recent multicenter validation, FDA-AF (FibriCheck, 12-lead reference): accuracy 98.5%, sensitivity 96.3%, specificity 99.3%. Sensitivity was reduced in darker skin tones and higher BMI, mitigated by technician verification. An emergency app has no technician in the loop. I read an abstract-level record only. ([source](https://vivo.weill.cornell.edu/display/pubid41266794)) (2025-11-20)
- **high:** The main cleared camera PPG product is scoped far from this use. FibriCheck K232804 is Class II, product code DXH, 21 CFR 870.2920, prescription use only, indicated for self-testing by patients diagnosed with or susceptible to AF for intermittent monitoring. It covers neither third-party use nor emergencies. Preventicus Heartbeats is reported as CE class IIa, and eBRAVE-AF used one-minute PPG followed by a confirmatory 14-day ECG patch (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34252387/, medium confidence, partly company sourced). ([source](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232804.pdf)) (2024-06-07)
- **medium:** PPG does not diagnose AF on its own. Reviews of the 2024 ESC guidance state that diagnosis needs ECG documentation, with PPG treated as screening or monitoring. I read secondary sources, not the ESC text. ([source](https://esc365.escardio.org/journal/75901)) (2024)
- **high:** AHA 2025 Part 7 does not have lay rescuers use a pulse check. An adult who is unresponsive with absent or abnormal breathing (including only gasping) is assumed to be in arrest, and lay recognition is "based primarily on level of consciousness and respiratory effort". All lay rescuers should give compressions for presumed arrest (Class 1, B-NR). Agonal breathing occurs in up to 40-60% of OHCA and is a common cause of lay misdiagnosis. AHA pages returned 403 to direct fetch, so this comes from search-engine excerpts of the AHA text; a clinician should confirm against the full chapter. ([source](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/adult-basic-life-support)) (2025-10)
- **high:** Even health care professionals get no more than 10 seconds for a pulse check (breathing and pulse together, 5-10 s) and must assume arrest if no definite pulse is felt, because AHA notes they take too long and struggle to judge it. ERC-based UK 2025 guidance likewise uses breathing rather than pulse, and says to assume arrest and start CPR when in doubt: https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/adult-basic-life-support-guidelines ([source](https://cpr.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-BLS-Adult-Healthcare-250701.pdf?sc_lang=en)) (2025)
- **high:** The error costs are asymmetric. ILCOR's draft review of laypeople giving CPR to patients not in arrest found under 1% unintentional injury and about 2% symptoms across five uncontrolled studies (1,031 patients) with no deaths due to CPR, and strongly recommends starting CPR for presumed arrest without concern for injury (low certainty; labeled draft for public comment). So missing an arrest is catastrophic while unnecessary CPR is low harm, and a test that can only argue against CPR has little upside. CPR can double or triple survival, yet only about 41% of OHCA adults get it before EMS: https://newsroom.heart.org/facts/cpr-cardiopulmonary-resuscitation-and-updated-guidelines-2025 ([source](https://costr.ilcor.org/document/unintentional-injury-by-laypersons-chest-compressions-to-patients-who-are-not-in-cardiac-arrest-fa-7670-tf-sr)) (literature search to 2024-09)
- **medium:** Human pulse palpation is itself poor, which is why it was removed for laypeople: first responders had about 90% sensitivity for pulselessness and 55% specificity, and only 15% reached a correct answer within 10 s (Eberle 1996, abstract-level via search). ([source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9025126/)) (1996)
- **medium:** I found no study of smartphone fingertip camera PPG in unconscious, cardiac-arrest, shocked or hypothermic patients. Closest analogues use wearable sensors and simulated pulselessness. Pixel Watch: 67.23% sensitivity in an arterial-occlusion simulation, tuned for specificity (1 errant call per 21.67 user-years), needs motion stillness, and is not intended for users with heart conditions. Khalili 2024 (fingertip IR PPG, occlusion): 93.7% of pulseless states with 0.4% false positives, but under idealized high-quality data (https://doaj.org/article/9c466d358e794dac88461b6b99c0546b). These answer whether a worn sensor's signal vanished, not whether a one-shot reading proves a stranger has a pulse (my inference). ([source](https://research.google/pubs/automated-loss-of-pulse-detection-on-a-commercial-smartwatch)) (2025)
- **medium:** Optical PPG can show a false pulse during arrest. BMJ Open 2019 (50 OHCA patients, 150 pulse checks, trained ED staff, smartwatch): the carotid site reached 100% sensitivity but 21.3% false positives; the wrist site reached 78.6% sensitivity and 100% specificity; manual palpation 78.6%/90.4%. ROSC was sometimes missed with irregular rhythms. Compressions also imprint on the signal: only 61% of pause data expected to show a pulse was artifact-free in 9 patients (https://em-consulte.com/article/1365790/on-detection-of-spontaneous-pulse-by-photoplethysm). The best algorithm reached 94% specificity and 69% sensitivity, using a compression rate from transthoracic impedance that a phone does not have (https://www.embs.org/tbme/?p=73, search-level). ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6367979)) (2019-02-03)
- **low:** Low perfusion and vasoconstriction attack the finger signal. Purpose-built finger oximeters in volunteers failed at about 25-47 mmHg radial systolic depending on method, up to about 58 mmHg with norepinephrine, with almost no plethysmographic flow just above failure (Severinghaus 1990, abstract-level). No phone-camera data found. App vendors warn against cold fingers (https://apps.apple.com/app/id1185234081, vendor statement only). Applying this to phones in shock or cold is my extrapolation. ([source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/2393137)) (1990)
- **medium:** False-pulse paths are plausible, though I found no study testing them directly on phones. (1) Rescuer's own finger: the app cannot tell whose finger it is, the rescuer is the one holding the phone, and a stressed rescuer reads as a strong fast pulse. (2) Motion: compressions at 100-120/min are 1.67-2 Hz, inside the normal heart-rate band, and a study states available apps are unreliable with artifacts present. (3) No liveness or identity check found in any source. A false pulse is the one output that could stop CPR. ([source](https://www.kom.tu-darmstadt.de/publications/GDG17-1)) (2017-01)
- **medium:** Delay is a hazard even when the reading is correct. FibriCheck requires a 60-second recording, six times the 10-second cap for professionals, with hands off the chest, and shortening analysis costs sensitivity. Survival is commonly quoted as falling 7-10% per minute without CPR or defibrillation (secondary attribution to Larsen et al., so verify before citing: https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Chain_of_Survival). ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11023210/)) (2024-04)
- **high:** ARKit and a separate capture session cannot share the camera. An Apple Media Engineer stated in Sept 2026 that ARKit acts as a client with its own AVCaptureSession and a second one you start steals from and interrupts the first. A later Apple correction in the thread concerned only CameraCaptureAccessory. Apple DTS said in 2022 that with AVCaptureMultiCamSession you cannot use ARKit and must build your own AR (https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/700542). Both are forum answers; I found no formal doc stating the rule. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847744)) (2026-09)
- **medium:** Multi-camera sessions exist on iPhone XS/XR and later (check isMultiCamSupported and supportedMultiCamDeviceSets), with hardwareCost required below 1.0, one session at a time, roughly two physical cameras as a rule of thumb, formats up to 60 fps binned, and a system pressure score (above 3 means only brief running). Limits are from WWDC 2019 and may have changed. That session does not mention torch, so torch in multi-cam is unverified. A 2026 developer report describes ARKit breaking alongside multi-cam, with no Apple reply: https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/822409 ([source](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/249/)) (2019)
- **high:** The decisive conflict is physical. A finger on the lens blocks that camera's view, and the phone must be pressed to a finger rather than aimed at a chest, so PPG and body-pointing AR are mutually exclusive even if the software allowed both (my reasoning). Torch during ARKit is reported to work through AVCaptureDevice if set shortly after session start, but only in unconfirmed developer posts with no Apple reply. The torch alone does not solve this, and exposure control via configurableCaptureDeviceForPrimaryCamera is nil on devices without an ultra-wide camera (https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/arkit/arconfiguration/configurablecapturedeviceforprimarycamera.json). Practical pattern: pause ARSession, run a plain AVCaptureSession for PPG, then resume. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/103211)) (2018-05 to 2019-04)
- **high:** The torch can fail in long sessions. isTorchAvailable can go false when the device overheats and needs to cool off, and maxAvailableTorchLevel may drop below 1.0 under thermal duress (https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/maxavailabletorchlevel.json). Streaming a VLM with AR at the same time adds thermal load (my inference). Lens and flash layout also varies by iPhone, so apps show per-model finger placement (vendor statements only). I found no primary data on fingertip heating. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/istorchavailable.json)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **high:** App Store guideline 1.4.1 requires disclosing data and methodology for health-measurement accuracy claims and rejects what cannot be validated. It bans sensor-only blood pressure, body temperature, glucose and blood oxygen claims. Heart rate is not on the banned list, but camera SpO2 must not creep into scope. Apps must remind users to consult a doctor, an awkward fit for an emergency flow. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)) (accessed 2026-11-05)
- **medium:** Use case verdict, awake patient with palpitations, lightheadedness or chest symptoms: the only defensible role. This patient can hold still, can be asked for their own finger, and matches the validated population. Value is a heart-rate estimate for the EMS report and dispatcher. Limits: under-reads in AF at high rates, and no direct validation in an acute emergency, so it is defensible as documentation, not as triage. A normal reading must never lower urgency. ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11023210/)) (2024-04)
- **low:** Use case verdict, post-ROSC: not defensible for lay use. Data come from trained staff with smartwatches and show missed ROSC with irregular rhythms, and post-arrest peripheral perfusion is likely poor (inference). A reassuring number could pull attention from watching breathing and responsiveness. Keep the lay instruction to monitor breathing and restart CPR if it stops. I could not retrieve AHA or ERC post-ROSC lay wording, so a clinician should confirm. ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6367979)) (2019-02-03)
- **medium:** Use case verdict, bystander stress: cut. No evidence it changes any action, and it takes the rescuer's hands and the camera away. Heart rate is a debated fatigue marker: a manikin crossover found 1-minute rotation reduced fatigue yet heart-rate change did not differ. A rotation timer does the job without measurement. ([source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7482023/)) (2020)
- **medium:** Non-contact rPPG from the AR camera is the obvious workaround and should be rejected. It avoids the lens conflict, but non-contact apps did worse even in calm patients (mean absolute error 7.1 and 8.1 bpm, r about 0.6) and it carries the same false-reassurance risk in a moving scene. ([source](https://esc365.escardio.org/journal/29450)) (2017)

## Legal and technical reality of an AI agent contacting 911 in the US, plus FDA, liability, privacy and App Store constraints on bystander emergency guidance

An autonomous AI 911 call is not viable. iOS has no US API for it: tel: requires confirmation, CallKit is VoIP-only, Emergency SOS has no public API, and silent SMS is impossible. Twilio can technically dial 911, but it routes by a registered address, not the patient's location, and its terms do not fit. Several states restrict automatic 911 dialers, and TCPA's emergency exception will not obviously cover false positives or tests. Every AI pilot found sits on inbound non-emergency lines. Build this instead: the human dials, the AI prepares a script and coaches, dispatcher instructions override the AI, and data pathways are optional add-ons. Two surprises matter for planning. Google's Gemini and Vertex terms prohibit medical advice and uses requiring regulatory clearance. FDA treats bystander-facing recommendations as device functions: static first aid is unregulated, hand placement and metronome match Class I, and CPR feedback software is Class II needing a 510(k). PPG in an emergency is not wellness. Same-phone camera and mic during a live 911 call is unverified. This is research, not legal advice.

### Recommendation

RECOMMENDED LAWFUL 911 DESIGN: the human dials, the AI prepares and coaches, and the AI never touches the 911 line.

1. Call path. First screen has one large Call 911 button that opens tel:911 and takes the system confirm. Teach the side-button SOS as the fallback. No auto-dial, no countdown-to-call, no backend call. If a second person is present, the app tells them to call while the first starts CPR.

2. Caller script. A large-text card that updates live: address and coordinates, what happened, patient state, actions taken, resources on hand. The human reads it aloud. An optional Read Aloud runs only on a user tap for hands-busy moments, plays through the room, and states that it is an AI assistant speaking for the caller. This is the least certain piece of the voice design, because it needs the device test below.

3. Dispatcher overrides AI. Tell the user to follow the dispatcher when instructions differ. The agent ducks or pauses when the user reports the dispatcher is speaking. Do not record the 911 call.

4. Text fallback. Pre-fill MFMessageComposeViewController to 911 with address and emergency, for users who cannot speak. The user taps send, and the app tells them a bounce-back means they must call. Do not route this through Twilio.

5. Two-phone mode as the reliable case: one phone on 911 speaker, the other running camera and AI. Single-phone mode must degrade cleanly to on-screen steps and a metronome if audio or camera is interrupted. Prototype this first against a non-emergency number; it is the largest technical unknown.

6. Optional pathways, after MVP and never replacing the call. Apply to RapidSOS to attach location and summary to the caller's number. Evaluate Noonlight for a user-tapped I cannot talk path staffed by human agents. Server-sent alerts to pre-consented emergency contacts are fine but are not a 911 channel.

7. EMS report. Generate it locally and show it on screen or as a QR code at handoff. Label it AI-generated and unverified, record observations and timestamps rather than diagnoses, send nothing automatically, delete by default after a short window.

8. Sentinel. Treat as a gate: the LLM may only select and phrase steps from a versioned, guideline-sourced protocol library; free-generated clinical directives are blocked; on model or network failure the deterministic protocol and metronome continue offline.

REGULATORY TIERS. Tier 0 is a clearly labeled non-public prototype. Tier 1 ships static guideline content and tutorials (Appendix A), call and alert features (Appendix B), and possibly hand placement plus metronome as a Class I CPR aid with registration and listing, subject to counsel. Tier 2 requires clearance: camera-based compression feedback (510(k), LIX), AI cardiac arrest recognition, PPG rhythm or pulse (likely 510(k) or De Novo). Cut PPG from any MVP that makes medical claims. Use FDA's Q-Submission process before building Tier 2, and engage FDA and 911-telecom counsel before any public release.

MODEL CHOICE. Resolve vendor terms before committing to Gemini Live or the OpenAI Realtime API. Get written permission, or restrict the cloud model to non-clinical tasks such as speech and intent mapped onto the fixed protocol, or use a self-hosted model after checking its license.

PRIVACY. Get user consent at onboarding, with separate explicit third-party AI consent per Apple 5.1.2(i). Prefer on-device vision. No face embeddings or voiceprints. Use zero-retention, no-training vendor contracts. Keep a visible capture indicator. Treat the patient as a non-consenting data subject: minimize, blur stored frames, and do not retain. Build Washington MHMDA and FTC breach compliance from day one. Submit from a legal entity. Ask Apple about 5.1.5 before relying on location for dispatch.

DEMO AND TEST. Compile the real emergency number only into release builds, with debug and demo builds pointing to a mock dispatcher (a teammate's phone or a Twilio number with scripted audio). Add a CI check that fails on 911 outside the release configuration. Use vendor sandboxes. Never dial 911 on stage. If a real validation call is ever required, arrange it first through the PSAP's non-emergency line and state it is a test. Include an App Review demo mode that cannot dial.

### Blockers

- A fully autonomous AI 911 call from an iPhone in the US is impossible with public APIs: tel: requires confirmation, CallKit is VoIP-only, LiveCommunicationKit's no-prompt path is EU default-dialer only, and I found no Emergency SOS API.
- A backend AI agent dialing 911 through Twilio would route by the number's registered address, not the patient's location, so help could go to the wrong PSAP or through a national call center. It also fits poorly with Twilio's Approved Uses and collides with state auto-dialer restrictions.
- Sending SMS to 911 without user action is not possible on iOS, and Twilio warns against using Programmable SMS for 911.
- An AI voice agent speaking directly on a 911 call has no standard, pilot or PSAP acceptance I could find, and a false positive falls outside the clearest reading of the TCPA emergency exception and consumes real responder resources.
- Gemini API and Vertex terms prohibit providing medical advice and any use requiring medical device clearance, so the named primary model path conflicts with the product unless Google agrees otherwise. Gemini API terms also require users to be 18+.
- Real-time AI bystander guidance from camera or vision cannot use FDA's CDS exclusion, since it is not HCP-facing, is time-critical, and analyzes images. Camera-based CPR quality feedback is a Class II function needing a 510(k).
- PPG feeding emergency decisions is a device function, not wellness, and risks Apple 1.4.1 rejection without validation. A fingertip reading on a phone also cannot be assumed to work on a collapsed patient; clinical validity needs separate review.
- Live coaching through the same phone during a cellular 911 call may fail, since Apple documents phone calls making audio hardware unavailable to capture and the Phone UI takes the foreground. Do not promise it until tested on devices.
- Any test or demo that actually reaches a PSAP can tie up emergency resources and risks penalties. Unscheduled test calls and automated test loops must be engineered out.

### Open questions

- Is this a hackathon demo or a product intended for public release? This sets the FDA tier, entity needs and vendor-terms risk. I did not verify FDA treatment of non-distributed prototypes.
- iOS only or cross-platform? Android's ACTION_DIAL limit is similar, but its camera and audio behavior during calls was not researched.
- Does iOS allow continued camera, mic and audio playback by a third-party app during an actual emergency call, and does it differ from an ordinary call? Testable on non-emergency numbers only, so a scheduled PSAP test or Apple DTS question may be needed.
- Is an AI hand-placement AR overlay still a Class I CPR aid under 21 CFR 870.5210, or does vision analysis push it higher? Needs FDA Q-Sub or a 513(g) request.
- Will Google or OpenAI grant written permission for this use, and do candidate open-weight models carry similar use restrictions? Not researched here.
- How does Apple apply guideline 5.1.5 to apps that convey location for emergency response? Existing safety apps suggest some tolerance, but I did not confirm this.
- RapidSOS and Noonlight commercial terms, small-developer acceptance, and Noonlight's handling of unresponsive users. Portals were gated or silent.
- Full 50-state survey of auto-dialer and false-911 statutes, and whether any reach a phone app rather than a hardwired device. I sampled 5 states.
- Does AHA 2025 guidance on pulse checks and telecommunicator CPR require specific script wording or rule out a PPG step? I could not retrieve the primary text.
- Applicability of Illinois BIPA exclusions, Texas CUBI and all-party recording statutes to emergency capture, plus insurance availability. Needs counsel.

## Clinical content and safety engineering for an AI + AR phone emergency guide: guideline steps, MVP scenarios, evidence, CPR audio, Sentinel design, EMS handoff

2025 AHA guidelines exist and are current. Adult targets are unchanged: 100-120/min, 2-2.4 in, full recoil, pauses under 10 s. The biggest finding is adverse: AHA 2025 says cognitive aids are NOT recommended for lay rescuers because they delayed CPR starts, and smartphone apps fall within that category. Time-to-first-compression must therefore be the product's top metric. LLM evidence argues against free-form generated instructions: omissions dominate, and users do worse than the models alone. Recommended MVP: adult cardiac arrest (hands-only CPR + AED) and life-threatening limb bleeding, with opioid overdose third. Recommended Sentinel: a deterministic on-device state machine as source of truth, allowlisted pre-rendered phrases, and an asynchronous LLM auditor that never gates speech. Use a local double-click metronome at 110/min, not a commercial song. Remove PPG from all CPR decisions. Treat phone accelerometer depth as unreliable. The EMS output should be a one-screen handoff card, not a NEMSIS record. Caveat: AHA full text was blocked (403), so clinical wording needs clinician verification against Parts 6, 7, 10 and 12.

### Recommendation

MVP SCENARIOS
1. Adult cardiac arrest: hands-only CPR + AED (must have). Highest stakes, strongest protocol, and metronome, timer and report all add value.
2. Life-threatening limb bleeding (Stop the Bleed steps, not its brand). Short protocol; the camera can help with tourniquet placement and the timestamp is valuable to EMS.
3. Opioid overdose/naloxone (only if time allows). Lay administration is endorsed, and it hands off into scenario 1, though it needs breaths.
For a hackathon, ship 1 and demo 2. Later: anaphylaxis second-dose timer, seizure timer, stroke checklist. Exclude choking from AI coaching (seconds matter); show a static card only. Infants, children and pregnancy get a triage gate, a static card and dispatcher deferral, not adult coaching.

ADULT ARREST FLOW
Two yes/no questions only: responsive? breathing normally (gasping = no)? No pulse check. Then 911 on speaker, AED fetch, compressions. Hard KPI: time to first compression no worse than dispatcher-only on manikin testing, because of the AHA cognitive aid recommendation. Any uncertainty defaults to CPR.

SENTINEL DESIGN (layered)
- L0 on-device deterministic protocol state machine, authored from guideline text and signed off by a clinician. It alone decides the next instruction; under 1 ms.
- L1 allowlist: in critical states the LLM can only emit say(phrase_id) or event tool calls; audio is pre-rendered and local. Model free text is never spoken in arrest states.
- L2 deterministic validators on any permitted free text: numbers, doses, banned acts (pulse check, stop CPR, diagnosis, death). Requires text before audio.
- L3 on-device retrieval returning approved snippets verbatim for side questions.
- L4 asynchronous LLM auditor out of band: compares transcript to state, may flag or trigger a correction phrase, never gates speech.
- Vendor guardrails are a backstop only.
- Vision and PPG outputs are hypotheses. Stopping CPR requires human confirmation of breathing, movement or EMS takeover. Failure mode: network or model loss degrades to L0 with local audio; Sentinel can never halt guidance.

CPR AUDIO
Local double-click metronome at 110/min from a sample-accurate source in the same AVAudioEngine as the agent voice, voice processing on, self-ducked but never muted during speech, mid-frequency timbre. 'Push hard, let it come up' every 60 s because of depth loss. Client-side voice gating so ticks do not trigger barge-in. Optional original or commissioned 110 BPM track; no commercial song. AED and dispatcher voices take priority.

SENSORS
Report rate only from vision or inertial data; no depth score unless validated, and never on a soft surface. PPG out of the arrest path entirely.

EMS REPORT
One-screen card plus read-aloud: witnessed, collapse time, CPR start and by whom, shocks with times, naloxone, tourniquet site and time, ROSC or responsiveness, what bystander reported. Tag each item as timer, sensor, bystander-reported or AI-inferred. Timeline first; IMIST-AMBO or MIST headings. NEMSIS vocabulary alignment only, no export in MVP.

BEFORE SHIPPING
Get a medical director, read Parts 6, 7, 10 and 12 in full, and run a manikin study against dispatcher-only.

### Blockers

- Free-form LLM voice giving resuscitation instructions is unsafe as stated: published failures include omissions and harmful directions, and streaming guardrails cannot retract audio already buffered or heard.
- PPG sending data to the VLM 'to decide what to do' is unsafe for arrest: AHA says lay rescuers should not rely on pulse checks, no smartphone-camera validation in low perfusion or arrest was found, a false pulse could stop CPR, and App Store Guideline 1.4.1 rejects health measurements whose accuracy cannot be validated.
- A commercial CPR beat song cannot be played without rights-holder licenses; App Store Guideline 4.5.2(ii) names this exact use, and MusicKit does not cover it.
- AHA 2025 says cognitive aids are not recommended for lay rescuers because they delay CPR. A multi-step app or AR setup before compressions directly conflicts with this unless testing shows no added delay.
- A single rescuer cannot hold a phone for AR while compressing with two hands. Live AR only works before compressions, with a second person, or with the phone propped up, and no live-body validation was found. Camera view and on-body accelerometer also conflict on a single phone.
- Phone accelerometer depth is not trustworthy when strapped or held or on soft surfaces (documented overestimation), so a depth score could falsely reassure.
- An LLM Sentinel as an in-line blocking gate will not fit the live budget: a compression beat at 110/min is 545 ms. It must be asynchronous, with deterministic layers doing the prevention.
- A cellular 911 call on the same iPhone is likely to interrupt the app audio session, potentially stopping the metronome and voice agent. Unverified on device, but the architecture must not assume coexistence. AI voice must also not compete with the dispatcher.
- App Store Guideline 5.1.5 says location-based APIs should not be used to provide emergency services. This needs review before any 911 or location agent is planned.
- A bystander app does not produce a NEMSIS ePCR record; it is an agency data standard. Promise a handoff card, not a NEMSIS report. 'Stop the Bleed' branding also needs permission.

### Open questions

- Is this a hackathon demo or a shipping product? That sets whether a medical director, clinical validation and regulatory work are required now.
- Who is the clinician who signs off the protocol state machine and phrase library?
- Need full text of AHA Parts 6, 7, 10, 12 for exact COR/LOE, the lay rescuer algorithm, sternum wording (lower half vs lower third), pediatric AED pads, and pregnancy lay steps. Can the user obtain institutional or direct browser access?
- Does the Part 12 cognitive aid statement apply to real-time interactive voice coaching, or mainly to printed and static aids? Ten small simulation studies leave room for interpretation; ask a resuscitation expert.

- On device, what survives an active cellular call: app playback, microphone, voice processing? Test with a non-emergency number only.
- What is the measured latency of an async LLM auditor and of the chosen realtime API? Are text-only or tool-call-only turns usable with Gemini Live and OpenAI Realtime in practice?

- Do AHA or ACS content reuse terms allow adapting their steps and figures? Not verified; author original wording and art, and check licensing.
- What are FDA device status, liability and Good Samaritan implications of the app? Not researched in this topic beyond noting App Store rules.

Agonal breathing is slow, irregular, ineffective gasping, reportedly present in up to 40-60% of out-of-hospital arrests (secondary summary of Part 7). Seizure-like movement can occur at arrest onset (UK guideline). ([source](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001369)) (2025-10-22)
- **medium:** Adult compressions: 100-120/min; depth at least 2 in (5 cm), with depths above 2.4 in (6 cm) flagged as excessive; full recoil with no leaning; pauses under 10 s. Firm surface, torso at about the rescuer's knee level; same technique for obesity. Hand position: heel of one hand with the other on top, lower half of the sternum per a UC Davis summary, but a secondary summary of Part 7 says lower 1/3. That discrepancy must be checked in Part 7 before setting the AR target. ([source](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/cppn/documents/pdfs/ucdh-cpr-summary.pdf)) (2025)
- **high:** Hands-only vs breaths: all lay rescuers should give compressions. For those trained and able it is reasonable to use 30:2, and AHA now states cycles of 30 and 2 are reasonable for lay rescuers. Telecommunicators are told to guide Hands-Only CPR for adults and conventional CPR with breaths for children, and the No-No-Go framework is reiterated. Part 1 retires the term 'rescue breaths' in favor of 'breaths'. ([source](https://cpr.heartandstroke.ca/resource/GuidelinesHighlightsEN2025)) (2025-10)
- **medium:** Lay rescuers should start CPR when arrest is presumed because risk to a person not in arrest is low. A systematic review cited by the Red Cross found unintentional injury in about 0.7%, none fatal (the 954 vs 1,031 denominator differs across sources). The 2020 AHA recommendation was Class 1; the 2025 wording was not verified. The safe default under uncertainty is to start compressions. ([source](https://guidelines.redcross.org/arc-2022-highlights/unintentional-injury-from-cpr-to-persons-not-in-cardiac-arrest/)) (2022)
- **high:** AED and pads: pads go on the bare chest, anterolateral or anteroposterior; the lateral pad sits in the mid-axilla, not too far forward. 2025 new: 'it might be reasonable to adjust the position of a bra instead of removing it' (women receive less public-access defibrillation). UK guidance: if the bra prevents correct placement, remove it. Resume compressions immediately after a shock. The AED's own diagrams and prompts are authoritative, so the app must not talk over them (my inference). ([source](https://cpr.heartandstroke.ca/resource/GuidelinesHighlightsEN2025)) (2025-10)
- **high:** Infants and children: lay rescuers start CPR without a pulse check. Depth at least 1/3 of chest, about 2 in for children (1 year to puberty) and about 1.5 in for infants; same rate; 30:2 for one rescuer and 15:2 for two (HCP-oriented table). 2025 removes the 2-finger technique: use 2 thumb-encircling hands, or the heel of 1 hand if the rescuer cannot encircle the chest. Pauses under 10 s. Arrest is usually respiratory, so breaths matter more. ([source](https://cpr.heartandstroke.ca/resource/GuidelinesHighlightsEN2025)) (2025-10)
- **low:** Pediatric AED: Red Cross 2022 says adult pads may be used on infants and children when pediatric pads are unavailable, as long as they do not touch, with anteroposterior placement under 8 years. Pediatric pads or an attenuator are preferred if available, per a study guide. The 2025 AHA Part 6 wording was not confirmed. ([source](https://guidelines.redcross.org/arc-2022-highlights/pad-size-and-placement-in-infants-and-children/)) (2022)
- **medium:** Pregnancy: standard CPR plus manual left lateral uterine displacement when fundal height is at or above the umbilicus; resuscitative delivery by 5 minutes is a hospital and EMS goal. I found no lay-specific steps. A secondary Canadian document treats displacement as an awareness point, not a routine step. Suggested lay behavior: standard CPR and AED, displacement only if a second person is free, and tell EMS she is pregnant. Choking in late pregnancy uses 5 chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts. ([source](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001380)) (2025-10-22)
- **high:** Choking, adult (2025 algorithm): severe signs are weak or absent cough, unable to speak, cyanosis, altered mental status, apnea. If absent, encourage coughing and keep checking. If present, activate EMS, then repeat 5 back blows followed by 5 abdominal thrusts until expelled or unresponsive. If unresponsive, start CPR beginning with compressions and check the mouth for a visible object before breaths. Children: same cycles. Infants: 5 back blows and 5 heel-of-1-hand chest thrusts; abdominal thrusts are not recommended. ([source](https://cpr.heartandstroke.ca/resource/GuidelinesHighlightsEN2025)) (2025-10)
- **high:** Severe bleeding: ACS Stop the Bleed teaches: always alert emergency services, apply pressure with hands, pack the wound and press, apply a tourniquet. Red Cross and DHS add: tourniquet about 2-3 in above the wound, between wound and heart, not on a joint, tighten until bleeding stops, note the time. Packing suits scalp, neck, shoulder, groin or back, or a limb with no tourniquet. AHA 2024 First Aid prefers a commercial tourniquet over an improvised one. ([source](https://www.stopthebleed.org/media/plpjtq5p/25_tr_stb_handout_4x6_acslogo.pdf)) (2024)
- **high:** Licensing: STOP THE BLEED is a registered trademark of the US Department of Defense, licensed to ACS, so do not brand with it without permission. ACS says the course skills portion must be completed in person, which implies the app cannot claim to substitute for that training. ([source](https://www.stopthebleed.org/about/faq/)) (2026)
- **high:** Anaphylaxis (2024 AHA/Red Cross First Aid): IM epinephrine by autoinjector is the main immediate treatment and first aid providers may assist self-administration. If there is no response to the first dose and EMS will take more than 5-10 min, a repeat dose may be considered; an estimated 7-18% need more than one dose. Red Cross includes nasal spray and says to follow the person's action plan. The guideline does not support the app diagnosing anaphylaxis. ([source](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001281)) (2024)
- **high:** Opioid overdose: 2025 AHA says naloxone should be given to people in respiratory arrest from suspected overdose (COR 1, LOE B-NR per excerpt) and lay rescuers can administer it. In cardiac arrest it 'may be reasonable' provided it does not interfere with standard resuscitation including high-quality CPR with breaths. SAMHSA: give it, call 911, support breathing, repeat every 2-3 min if no response, recovery position once breathing; it wears off in 30-90 min and more than one dose may not be needed. ([source](https://library.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/overdose-prevention-response-kit-pep23-03-00-001.pdf)) (2023)
- **medium:** Stroke: the action is recognition and 911. AHA First Aid supports a scale such as FAST. Sources conflict on whether AHA has moved to BE-FAST; one 2026 summary cites 'FAST or BE-FAST'. A pilot RCT found lower 30-day retention of all symptoms with BE-FAST (24.4% vs 51.1%), though it may catch more posterior strokes. I could not confirm a formal AHA adoption. There is little for the phone to do beyond a checklist, 911, and logging last-known-well time. ([source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12307487/)) (2025)
- **high:** Seizure (CDC): stay, time it, clear objects, turn on side, cushion the head, loosen neck clothing. Do not restrain, put anything in the mouth, give mouth-to-mouth during the seizure, or give food or water until fully alert. Call 911 if over 5 minutes, repeated, trouble breathing or waking, injury, in water, first seizure, diabetes with loss of consciousness, or pregnancy. AHA 2024 adds infants under 6 months and no return to baseline in 5-10 min. Low-risk, timer-driven, post-MVP candidate. ([source](https://www.cdc.gov/epilepsy/first-aid-for-seizures/index.html)) (2024)
- **high:** Dispatcher-assisted CPR is the benchmark to beat. ILCOR (2018 CoSTR, strong recommendation on very low certainty evidence): bystander CPR rate OR 3.10 (RR 2.00, 269 more per 1,000); good neurological survival OR 1.70; survival to discharge OR 1.23 (CI 0.99-1.53, not significant). A later review finds DA-CPR better than no CPR but worse than bystander-initiated CPR. Video dispatch 'may be reasonable' per 2025 Part 4; less than 20% in one study actually received it, partly because it needs two bystanders. ([source](https://costr.ilcor.org/document/emergency-care-dispatcher-instruction-in-cpr)) (2018-10-08)
- **medium:** LLMs fail in ways that matter here. Birkun's scoping review (2017-2025) found flaws in 89.5% of studies of GenAI layperson resuscitation and first aid advice, with incomplete instructions most common (the author led four included studies; search ended May 2025). Bing and Bard fully met checklist criteria in only 9.5-11.4% of responses in 2023. Yau et al. (late 2023 free chatbots) found about 50% accuracy and completeness. Newer models may be better, but no current-model CPR audit was found. ([source](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/dtfptcxxfk)) (2025-12)
- **high:** Human-LLM interaction is a distinct failure point. Bean et al. (Nat Med 2026, n=1,298 RCT): LLMs alone identified the condition in 94.9% of scenarios, but participants using them identified relevant conditions in under 34.5% and picked correct disposition under 44.2%, no better than controls. Design implication (my inference): do not make scene assessment depend on a stressed user describing symptoms to an LLM; ask fixed yes/no questions. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-04074-y)) (2026-02)
- **medium:** Triage reliability is contested but concerning. Ramaswamy et al. (Nat Med 2026) found ChatGPT Health under-triaged 52% of gold-standard emergencies across 960 responses. A preprint critique argues the rate depends strongly on exam-style format (forced A/B/C/D gave 0-24% for some models on an asthma case vs 100% free text), but it is a partial replication via APIs. Either way the app should not let a model make the arrest decision. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04297-7)) (2026-05)
- **medium:** Closest published precedent: U-CARE (JACC Case Rep 2026), a smartphone system using a YOLOv11 vision model and a constrained LLM mapping metric deviations to predefined validated BLS templates, with no detailed correction at low confidence and RAG for non-emergency questions. Reported rate MAPE 1.82%, depth MSE 0.32 cm2, 28 ms per frame, 400 ms end to end, and less harm than GPT-4.1 and Claude Haiku in human evaluation. Limits: manikin videos of trained professionals, preclinical, cloud dependent, no hand placement numbers. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaccas.2026.110026)) (2026-09)
- **medium:** Real-time feedback devices improve CPR quality; outcome benefit is inconsistent. AHA 2025 recommends them during training for both lay rescuers and professionals. Clinical meta-analyses conflict: 2020 (11 studies, n=4,851) did not confirm effectiveness; 2023 (13 studies, n=17,600) found ROSC benefit in-hospital but not out-of-hospital; 2024 reported ROSC OR 1.26 and survival OR 1.52, mostly observational. A scoping review notes some devices reduced depth or increased workload and distraction. I did not retrieve the ROC PRIMED trial. ([source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11477487/)) (2024)
- **medium:** Phone accelerometer: best published validation is Stumpf et al. (AJEM 2022): phone placed on a manikin's chest at the compression point, 60 sessions of 30 s, depth within +/-4 mm (under 10% error), count ICC 0.92 against a standard tool. That placement is manikin-only and not obviously feasible under a rescuer's hands on a real person. I found no real-patient phone study. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2022.06.045)) (2022)
- **medium:** Strapped or held phones are less trustworthy for depth. A registered trial states a prior armband study overestimated depth because phone weight amplified inertial movement, and a paper cites significantly more error for forearm than back-of-hand sensors. Results for hand vs armband PocketCPR positions were not found. Rate is more robust than depth. Smartwatches look better: +/-3.8 mm and 0.8 compressions per 5 s (Rao 2025, n=83), bias -0.31 cm with wide limits (de Borba 2026), all on manikins. ([source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02964832)) (2016)
- **high:** Soft surfaces break accelerometer depth: a sternum-only accelerometer read 54-56 mm when the manikin actually moved 35-42 mm on mattresses (Beesems 2014), and about 103 mm vs 56 mm on a foam mattress (Yu 2017). A person in bed is a common real scenario, so the app must not reassure on depth from inertial data. Coach by rate, plus 'push hard', and move the patient to a firm surface if feasible. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2014.08.005)) (2014)
- **medium:** PPG conflicts with guidance and with the store. AHA says lay rescuers cannot reliably determine a pulse and should not delay CPR. My search found no paper testing smartphone camera PPG in low perfusion or arrest. The only loss-of-pulse validation found used a wrist wearable in 44 ICU patients with non-shockable arrests (Lancet Reg Health Eur 2026). App Store Guideline 1.4.1 says apps whose accuracy or methodology cannot be validated will be rejected. A false 'pulse present' could stop compressions, and it takes a hand off the chest. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)) (2026)
- **medium:** Phone audio could passively detect agonal breathing in research: Chan et al. (npj Digit Med 2019) reported 97.24% sensitivity and 99.51% specificity using 911 audio, but called it proof of concept with limited data and not tested in noisy compressions. Interesting post-MVP assist for the 'breathing normally?' question; must only push toward CPR, never away from it. ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6584582)) (2019)
- **high:** Song licensing: App Store Guideline 4.5.2(ii) is explicit: 'if you want your app to play a specific song at a particular moment' you must contact rights-holders for permission (such as synchronization or adaptation rights) and assets; MusicKit is not a replacement. Users must also initiate Apple Music playback with standard controls, and it requires a subscription for catalog access. Composition and master are separate rights, and PRO blanket licenses do not clear sync. Budget and timing make this a poor fit for an emergency feature. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)) (2026)
- **medium:** Metronome vs song: metronomes reliably improve rate in manikin studies but depth can fall, especially in lay participants (BestBETs: Park 2013 phone speaker, untrained n=67, more shallow compressions, p=0.035; a reported 111.9 vs 96.7/min, 97% vs 14.7% in range, figures from a search excerpt). Songs are at best comparable: Hafner 2012 was n=15 providers, and Hong 2016 (61 metronome, 68 song) suggests better 6-month retention, which matters for pre-training, not live guidance. No trial of real arrests found. ([source](https://www.bestbets.org/?p=5955)) (2013)
- **medium:** Better metronome evidence: RITMICO (multicenter RCT, n=503, manikin) used a double-click metronome signaling compression and end of release; depth +0.1 cm (+2.5%) and recoil velocity +6 mm/s (+1.5%), statistically significant but small. Verbal motivation every 60 s plus phone metronome improved depth (Gehlen 2025, also Plata 2021: 59 mm, rate 99/min vs 78/min telephone only). Haptic at 110/min raised in-range from about 44% to 91% (Yilmaz 2026). No study of a voice count vs metronome was found. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resplu.2025.100867)) (2025)
- **medium:** iOS audio: with voice processing enabled, an Apple engineer states it ducks audio not part of the voice processing output, including a secondary stream from your own app, whose volume is significantly reduced, and that the pipeline is distinct and band-limited with separate output level. voiceProcessingOtherAudioDuckingConfiguration (iOS 17+) sets ducking level. So the metronome should be mixed into the same AVAudioEngine output as the agent voice, not played from a separate player (the last step is my inference, needs device testing). ([source](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/829753)) (2026-06)
- **medium:** iOS echo options: Apple voice processing automatically applies echo cancellation. setPrefersEchoCancelledInput (iOS 18.2+) is the alternative, valid only with playAndRecord and default mode, on certain 2024 or later iPhones, and tuned for a wider range of signals; check isEchoCancelledInputAvailable. Implication: voice processing is the portable path. A WebRTC stack that owns the audio unit may force the metronome into a separate, ducked stream; verify this in a spike (inference). ([source](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/setprefersechocancelledinput(_:).md)) (2026)
- **medium:** Phone calls may kill the coaching stack on one phone. Apple: incoming calls interrupt the active audio session; setPrefersNoInterruptionsFromSystemAlerts only helps for banner-style calls until accepted, and has no effect in full-screen style. An accepted call interrupts the session. An Apple DTS comment and VoIP guides say cellular calls outrank app sessions. I did not find a primary source on microphone behavior during a cellular call. Must spike on a device using a non-emergency test number, never real 911. ([source](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/setprefersnointerruptionsfromsystemalerts(_:).md)) (2026)
- **high:** Vendor streaming guardrails cannot prevent speech. OpenAI's Agents SDK realtime guide says output guardrails are debounced over accumulated text and transcript, a trip interrupts and cancels, and 'some audio may already be buffered when the tripwire fires'. The TS default is every 100 characters, about 18 words or 7 s of speech at 150 wpm (my arithmetic). A GitHub issue reported Python did not auto-block at that time. These are useful as a backstop only. ([source](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/realtime/guide/)) (2026)
- **medium:** Text-first gating is possible. OpenAI Realtime supports output modality audio or text per response or session (not both); audio includes a transcript. Gemini Live's main voice setting is AUDIO, with TEXT aimed at transcription; Gemini Live supports tool calls. A tool-call-only or text-only turn fed to local pre-rendered audio is therefore a plausible pattern, though the docs I saw did not show this exact pattern. The API researcher should confirm. ([source](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime-conversations)) (2026)
- **medium:** Non-zero error rates are the norm even in favorable settings: Asgari et al. (npj Digit Med 2025) measured 1.47% hallucination and 3.45% omission across 12,999 clinician-annotated note sentences, and one review reports hallucination rates ranging up to 61.6%. A systematic review reports RAG reduces hallucinations by 30-50%, not to zero. RAG helps but cannot be the safety guarantee for imperative instructions, and RAG does not catch omissions or sequencing errors. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-01670-7)) (2025-05-13)
- **low:** Latency math: at 110/min one beat is 545 ms. Any cloud round trip plausibly spans at least one beat, so cadence, counts and critical prompts must be local. Design targets (mine, not a standard): state transition and allowlisted prompt under 100 ms; side-question acknowledgment locally immediately and answer under 1-1.5 s; async auditor flag under 3 s. An in-line LLM checker latency was not sourced; I estimate hundreds of ms to seconds on a network. Measure it in the spike.
- **medium:** NEMSIS: v3.5.1 (January 2025) is the latest dictionary found; the v3 standard is an agency ePCR schema. The v3.5.1 eArrest schema has no dedicated pre-arrival CPR element; eArrest.05, .06 and .08 are absent. Useful fields: eArrest.04 witnessed by, .07 AED use prior to EMS, .12 any ROSC, .14 arrest time (approximate allowed), .19 initial CPR time, .20 who first initiated CPR, .21 who first applied AED, .22 who first defibrillated. Align vocabulary only; I found no route for a bystander app to submit records. ([source](https://nemsis.org/media/nemsis_v3/release-3.5.1/DataDictionary/APIs/EMSDataSetAPI/eArrest_v3_xsd.html)) (2025-01)
- **medium:** Handoff priorities: a 2025 modified Delphi (10 experts) built a 13-item EMS-to-ED cardiac arrest checklist and put witnessed arrest, bystander CPR and estimated downtime first; other items include age, location or cause, rhythms, ROSC, shocks, medications, code status. Video review found key information can go uncommunicated in up to 86% of EMS arrest handoffs. This is EMS-to-ED. I found no study of bystander-to-EMS handoff or a validated '30 second' format, so the 30-second card is an extrapolation. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acepjo.2025.100071)) (2025-04)
- **medium:** Formats: MIST (mechanism, injuries, signs, treatment) suits trauma but I found no strong evidence base for it. IMIST-AMBO has the best evidence found: more information per handover, fewer ED questions, shorter duration and fewer repetitions in a NSW quality study, similar in a Canadian trauma center. SBAR is common and adapted as ISBAR, and a review compares it with IMIST-AMBO; no randomized outcome comparison found. A timeline with counts suits arrest better than SBAR prose (my judgment). ([source](https://qualitysafety.bmj.com/content/21/8/627)) (2012)

# Adversarial critique

# Adversarial review of the three drafts

**Verdict:** Use fastest-demo as the skeleton, safety-first's gate as the control layer, and latency-architecture's audio and concurrency internals. All three drafts share two serious gaps:
- None handles a bystander who says "yes, he's breathing" about agonal gasps.
- The two designs built on a single always-on `playAndRecord` audio graph (fastest-demo and latency-architecture) stop working during a real same-phone call.

I ran only read-only checks. Nothing was tested on a device. Legal points are not legal advice.

## Ranked findings

### Critical

**1. The "yes, breathing" branch can miss an arrest (all three; harm)**
- **Problem:** Every draft defines the "no" and "unsure" paths, but none defines what follows "yes". Latency's rule that lowering urgency needs "human confirmation" doesn't help, because that "yes" is the human confirmation.
- **Evidence:**
  - The brief puts agonal breathing in up to 40-60% of arrests.
  - AHA says to assume arrest with abnormal breathing ([Part 7 mirror](https://u1-cd-cpr.sc.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/adult-basic-life-support)), but the part I read gives no script for an uncertain or mistaken bystander.
  - A dispatcher trial cut missed arrests from 28% to 18.8% by adding breath-count and sound questions, treating gaps of 10 s or more as a marker ([secondary report](https://www.mdedge.com/content/breathing-check-improves-detection-cardiac-arrest), search excerpt).
  - Missed calls involved words like "snoring" and "slow" ([Swor 2024](https://scholarlyworks.corewellhealth.org/emergency_medicine_articles/219), search excerpt).
- **Fix (my proposal, needs clinician approval):** "Yes" never ends the flow. It moves to a watch state:
  - 911 stays prompted.
  - The bystander marks each breath for 10 s.
  - The app re-asks at a short interval.
  - No reply defaults to CPR.

**2. Fastest-demo leaves model audio on during recognition (fastest-demo; latency partly; harm)**
- **Problem:** `SpeechGate` mutes only `placeHands`, `compressions`, `pads` and `analyze`. Free-form model speech stays audible in `intakeResponsive` and `intakeBreathing`, where a wrong "he sounds fine" does the most damage. Validators read transcripts after the audio has played. Latency's `modelAllowed` for "non-critical states" is never defined.
- **Related gap:** Proactive audio is permanent on 3.8 Live and the model "can proactively decide not to respond" ([capabilities](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/live-api/capabilities)). An intake that depends on `report_finding` can therefore stall silently, and fastest-demo sets no timeout.
- **Fix:** Gate closed by default, opened only by an allowlist. Nothing opens on an arrest-capable path. Add safety-first's 10 s default to CPR.

**3. A same-phone call breaks the `playAndRecord` graph, and Apple documents it (fastest-demo and latency; physical)**
- **Evidence:** [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/setactive:error:.md) says `record` or `playAndRecord` activation fails with `InsufficientPriority` when another app hosts a call, and an app can't capture the call's microphone. It also says an app "may activate a session with category playback" during a call.
- **Consequences:**
  - AHA's lone-rescuer order is call first, then CPR (COR 1). Following it leaves latency's "rebuild the graph" recovery unable to reactivate its `playAndRecord` session, so it retries until the call ends.
  - Fastest-demo's mock sheet hides this entirely.
  - Safety-first's `.playback` floor is on the right side of the rule.
  - Camera use is prohibited in the background ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturesession/interruptionreason/videodevicenotavailableinbackground.md)) and ARKit is interrupted there ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arsessionobserver/sessionwasinterrupted(_:).md)). The brief says the Phone UI takes the foreground.
  - An accepted call may suspend the app ([archived guide](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Audio/Conceptual/AudioSessionProgrammingGuide/HandlingAudioInterruptions/HandlingAudioInterruptions.html)).
  - I found no API to put a cellular call on speaker (unverified), so instruct "tap Speaker" before dialing.
- **Fix:** Build two audio modes. Use voice processing only when no call is active. During any call, including one already active at launch, use `.playback` with metronome and clips, no mic and no model uplink.
- **Day 1 spike against a teammate's phone:**
  - Is `.playback` audible during a cellular call?
  - Does `UIBackgroundModes: audio` keep it alive?
  - Real emergency-call behavior may still differ and stays unverified.

**4. Metronome collides with the AED, and the controls need free hands (all three; harm, physical)**
- **Problem:**
  - Safety-first pauses on an "AED talking" tap for at most 20 s, then auto-resumes.
  - Latency relies on a "Dispatcher is talking" tap.
  - Fastest-demo "goes quiet" with no defined trigger.
  - A rescuer compressing with both hands can't tap, and the tap and 2 s hold controls may fail with wet or gloved fingers (my inference).
- **Evidence:** The ZOLL AED 3 has its own [metronome and voice prompts](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1226671/Zoll-Aed-3.html?page=11). A [product description](https://www.stjohnwa.com.au/first-aid-kits-and-supplies/defibrillators/details/0743P?show=12) reports the same for the Philips FRx (lower confidence).
- **Risk:** Two out-of-phase clicks, or a click resuming while the AED says not to touch, could invite contact during analysis or shock (my inference). The 20 s limit has no source.
- **Fix:**
  - Once pads are on, the phone metronome stays off by default and the AED is the authority.
  - A local keyword can pause audio only and can never exit a state.
  - Measure timing on two or three trainer models.

### High

**5. The live caller card could send EMS to the wrong address (all three; harm, my inference)**
- **Problem:** A stressed caller may read a reverse-geocoded address off the card as fact.
- **Evidence:** iPhone has shared location with 911 centers on a native call since iOS 12 ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2018/06/apple-ios-12-securely-and-automatically-shares-emergency-location-with-911/)), where the center supports it. The card can therefore contradict better data. Guideline [5.1.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) also says location APIs "shouldn't be used to provide emergency services".
- **Fix:** Label it "Approximate, confirm", show the accuracy radius, and tell the caller to give any address they know first. Read Aloud never speaks an unconfirmed address.

**6. A demo build can be mistaken for the real thing (fastest-demo and latency; harm)**
- **Problem:** Safety-first found this one. Fastest and latency swap the number by build config, so a demo install can dial a teammate in a real emergency.
- **Fix:** Combine safety-first's separate name, icon and "will not call 911" banner with fastest-demo's build-phase check. Remove the build from any daily phone after the demo.

**7. The EMS card can state false negatives (all three; harm)**
- **Problem:** With hands busy, a shock may never be tapped, so the log could read as "0 shocks". "Metronome started" is not "CPR started", yet it would carry a trustworthy `timer` tag.
- **Fix:** Show "not recorded" rather than zero, and "coaching started" rather than "CPR started".

**8. Deterministic scripts dropped the brief's clinical steps (all three; harm)**

| Missing item | Support |
|---|---|
| Child, infant, drowning and pregnancy gate | Brief recommends static cards and dispatcher deferral |
| Roll flat onto back, firm surface | Firm surface is COR 2a |
| Switch compressors about every 2 minutes | COR 2a |
| "Push hard" every 60 s | Brief cites depth loss with metronomes |
| Second person calls while first compresses | Fastest's own script has the Rescuer dial with a Helper present |

**10. Patient privacy is exposed in the frame and audio paths (all three; legal)**
- **Problem:**

**11. The vendor and FDA gates are coupled, and the Class I route is doubtful (all three; legal)**
- **Evidence:** [Gemini terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms) bar providing medical advice and any use "overseen by or requires clearance or approval from a medical device regulatory agency". On my reading, the second limb is independent of the first.
- **Consequences:**
  - Safety-first lists FDA and vendor terms as separate gates, but a cleared or FDA-regulated product could itself trigger the Gemini bar. A written answer from Google must cover both limbs.
  - Safety-first's correction holds. [870.5210(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/870.5210) describes use by "persons professionally trained in CPR", so the brief's lay Class I tier is not established.
  - Public pitches count too: [21 CFR 801.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/801.4) treats "oral or written statements" as evidence of intended use. How that applies to a demo video is unverified.
  - The same terms bar Robotics Models from safety-critical healthcare work, so drop the brief's Robotics-ER "points" idea.

**12. "One phone" only works with two people (fastest-demo; latency partly; physical)**
- **Problem:** The fastest-demo script needs a Helper holding the phone while the Rescuer compresses. Latency disables the overlay for a lone rescuer, but no draft says how the app knows the rescuer is alone.
- **Fix:**
  - State this openly in the pitch.
  - Show the ring only if the torso is already in view, for at most 3 s.
  - Stop AR when the phone is put down, to shed heat.

**13. The launch target starts the clock too late (all three; harm)**
- **Problem:** "Under 1 s" is measured from launch. It ignores unlocking, finding the app, and the fact that the app must already be installed. Nobody should download an app mid-emergency.
- **Fix:**
  - Measure from collapse, phone locked.
  - Teach side-button SOS at onboarding.
  - Install speech assets at onboarding, because evidence says `SpeechAnalyzer` models [need a one-time download](https://dev.to/arshtechpro/wwdc-2025-the-next-evolution-of-speech-to-text-using-speechanalyzer-6lo) (third-party source).

## Decisions only you can make, in order

1. **Is this a private demo or a public product, and by what date?** This sets everything below.
2. **Can the model be silent on arrest paths?** It would hear and understand, while only approved local phrases speak. This replaces your talking voice agent there. I recommend yes.
3. **Do you accept the one-phone limit?** A lone rescuer on one phone gets at most the dispatcher plus a local metronome, if that survives testing. The AR and AI story then centers on a second person or second phone.
4. **Do you ratify the cuts?** PPG out of all decisions, no AI on the 911 line, no commercial song. Also decide whether to commission a 110 BPM track.
5. **What is the scope?** Adult arrest only, or adding limb bleeding, with static cards and 911 for everything else.
6. **Do you want on-device compression-rate feedback in the demo?** It restores your "realtime feedback" idea but needs clear labeling, and it is Class II software if shipped.
7. **Who reviews the clinical content?** A CPR instructor for a demo, a named medical director for a product.
